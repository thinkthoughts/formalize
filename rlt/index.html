02 / A working theorem-proving workflow

Check. Explain. Trace.

Three implemented commands expose distinct parts of the proof record. They can be used on the same mathematical statement without treating a proof verdict as a guarantee of faithful translation.

rlt check

Check the derivation

Replay every step under explicitly registered axioms and assumptions. Unsupported steps are refused.

rlt explain

Explain what was checked

Show pinned source, exact formal statement, proof result, and separately attributed semantic and physical correspondence assessments.

rlt deps

Trace the mathematics

Inspect cited theorems, definitions, axioms, assumptions, proof hashes, and citation-resource budgets.

Source-faithfulness, mathematical validity and empirical applicability have different verification procedures. Lean comparisons check corresponding formal propositions independently; their interpretation against the original writing remains an explicit review.

03 / Specification and trust

Four layers. Explicit correspondence.

A mathematical statement is written, interpreted under a registered convention, and checked. Any claim about the physical world requires separately registered evidence.

01

Written expression

Preserve the exact expression and its source location. Different inscriptions can denote equal values.

02

Convention

Declare the interpretation of notation, definitions and assumptions, with content hashes and sources.

03

Checked relationship

Kernel verification determines what follows from the registered formal premises and proof steps.

04

Physical correspondence

Local observations and evidence are recorded separately where the statement concerns physical referents.

An axiom's hash authenticates its recorded content, not its truth. An accepted formal theorem does not automatically establish that its source was translated faithfully or that it describes an observed physical event.

Reading points: math0 · math5 · math29

These original reading-point identifiers remain in the registry with their documented meanings. The user-facing mathematical demonstrations use ordinary names such as Modulo 30. The reading records retain the broader interpretive context from which RLT developed.
