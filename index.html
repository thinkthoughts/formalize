The Frontier Theorem Prover: RLT

Formal proof. Readable specifications. Evidence of reality.

Lean 4 checks proofs of precisely stated propositions using dependent type theory. Reading Level Test (RLT) is an independent first-order proof kernel that checks explicit derivations and tracks the conventions, assumptions, and written expressions to which they belong.

The research question is correspondence: when do distinct formal statements express the same relationship, and when does a claim about a physical system have evidence beyond a formally valid proof?

From proof to physical correspondence

The research has a common discipline: keep a written claim, its formal consequences, and evidence about the world distinguishable and linked.

1. Read the claim

Preserve the exact expression, conventions, scope, and assumptions. Identify what is being asserted, not merely what sounds plausible.

2. Check the relationship

Use RLT or Lean within its respective logic. Record the theorem, proof dependencies, axioms, and source revision.

3. Examine the evidence

For physical claims, ask for measurements, methods, units, uncertainty, and implementation correspondence. Formal proof alone is not a sensor.

Future coordination · physical specifications

Carbon dioxide, matter, and clean datacenters

The next meeting point for technical and nontechnical RLT is carbondioxide.now. Its proposed subject is accurate physical claims: for example, atoms in a measured molecule are understood through physical evidence and chemical relationships, rather than an invented origin story. Energy use, emissions, and datacenter operations likewise require measurements and clearly stated boundaries.

This is a research direction, not a claim that the physical specifications or a clean-datacenter audit are already complete.
