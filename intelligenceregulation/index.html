RLT Audits · Case 001

IntelligenceRegulation

A reading-level audit of intelligence, a feared future, formal verification, and claims about real systems.

The human question comes first

“How can we develop intelligence at a manageable rate worldwide.”

Victor Rielly's repository description frames the work around a fear: “if I don't do it they will and there will be no more me.” Those words document a human concern. They do not establish that the future is zero, that any person disappears, or that a speculative outcome has already occurred.

RLT asks: What exactly is written? What conventions give the statement meaning? Which relationships have been proved? What physical evidence supports claims about running systems?

What the project implements

IntelligenceRegulation develops Hashline, a research prototype for zero-knowledge proofs of AI inference. Its P1 design aims to keep prompts, outputs, and intermediate activations private while using public model weights. This is a specific cryptographic goal, separate from measuring how intelligence develops globally.

The four RLT layers

1. Convention

Define what “intelligence,” “manageable,” “private,” and “verified” mean in each local claim.

2. Written expression

Preserve the author's wording, exact Lean declarations, repository commits, and stated assumptions.

3. Proved relationship

State precisely which formal conclusions follow from which premises, with kernel checks and theorem links.

4. Physical record

Keep actual execution logs, hashes, measured behavior, and evidence about the implemented system distinct from proofs.

From origin stories to observed reality

The phrase “there will be no more me” can motivate research. It is not an empirical measurement. Human beings are alive now, speaking, reading, writing, and exhaling CO₂. These are observable local conditions, not deductions from an imagined zero future.

For clean datacenters, the corresponding nonfiction question is concrete: What computation ran, under which human authorization, and what energy, emissions, and water impacts were actually measured? Cryptographic proofs can address specified computational relationships. Scientific measurement must address the physical operations. RLT keeps those statements and their evidence connected without confusing them.

Next research target

Construct one auditable AI-inference reading point connecting a written safety or sustainability claim to its exact formal premises, the implemented computation, and local physical measurements. Use the existing review evidence as a starting point; do not label undeveloped protocol guarantees as completed proofs.
